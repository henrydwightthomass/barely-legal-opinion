New Kid on the Block

When does loyalty become blind allegiance?

By Henry Thomas August 2026 Issue 001 Politics
001

Old enough to vote.
Young enough to live with the consequences.

For a considerable amount of time, America has had two parties, which we recognize as most prevalent: the Republican and Democratic parties. As times have changed, we’ve seen an increase in independent politics, more moderation on both sides, democratic socialists, and people who push boundaries, ideas, and ideologies in ways we have never seen before. Change is good, right? Most people would say that diversity of thought is what makes our country our country, but among these new ideas, we must discuss the biggest, most radical change: what I would call the New Kid On The Block. MAGA, Make America Great Again, Trumpism, whatever you want to call it, is one of the most polarizing, radical, and loyal movements we have seen in the last couple of decades, possibly in the history of our country. When things considered abnormal become frequent, abnormality becomes normality, and that normality can create a sense of numbness in the general public. I would argue that this is not a good thing. If our country pledges allegiance to a leader, no matter their actions, the numbness is inevitable, and the normality of unjust, unconstitutional power becomes something people will bat an eye at. So let's take a look at it: when does loyalty become blind allegiance? And how exactly does it happen?

First, we must look at the original principles and the things that were run on to get him here to his 2nd term in the first place. One of his biggest running points was “No New Wars”. The President in his campaign constantly self-proclaimed himself as the only modern president to have started no new wars, and even though this statement was controversial in itself to some, he ran on the fact that he would keep us out of unneeded conflict and on multiple occasions told the people we wouldn't be in wars period. He constantly got on Biden for his involvement in Ukraine and condemned these actions, and during his 2015 campaign constantly called wars in the Middle East “stupid”. We are now in a war with Iran, in which the President has approximately stated 32-38 times that this war has ended, but as I’m typing this, we are currently still in this conflict. Conflicts always have consequences, and the consequences of our conflict in Iran are worth noting. By July 21st, the Pentagon had already estimated the cost of the war at $37.5 billion, with the total continuing to rise. The main concern, though, has been the immediate impact it has had on the people of our country. Restricted traffic through the Strait of Hormuz caused gas prices to skyrocket, hitting a single-month high of over $4.50 at the retail pump. Rising diesel prices also increased freight and trucking costs, which affected the cost of goods and food for people across the country. This rolls right into one of his next big running points: lowering the price of gas, even mentioning he would lower it to $2.00 a gallon. On top of this, he talked about the impact of inflation on common goods and promised this would be no more, but by July 2026, consumer prices were about 5% higher than they had been in January 2025, while prices were rising at a 3.4% annual rate. Energy prices alone were 14.7% higher than a year earlier, with gasoline up nearly 25%. Blind allegiance, right? No new wars, involvement in the Middle East is stupid, drastically lower gas prices, right? When all those fail, though, the blind allegiance becomes extremely prevalent, and the “Well, at least we don’t have a liberal in office.” “Kamala would have us in a way worse situation.” “He never promised to be perfect.” kicks in, and the excuses and dodging responsibility and reality itself becomes the reality. These are some of the economic and foreign promises that really stood out in his 2024 campaign, but this is not where the line ends. You cannot build the allegiance and loyalty MAGA has on economic or foreign promises alone; this is where the hot-topic promises come in.

Immigration, as soon as the word is mentioned at a dinner table or in a public space, is bound to spark differing opinions and heated arguments, making it one of the most significant topics in our political climate today. So obviously, Trump was very clear about his plans. Deport the people in the U.S. who are undocumented or not legal, focusing on the worst of the worst. But MAGA has never been able to take a topic and not polarize it to the max, and this is what they did. Trump would refer to these people he wanted to be deported with dehumanizing words such as “animals,” stating these people were not humans, but animals; he also called them rapists and stated they were poisoning the blood of our country. The insensitive, radical comments we have seen from him go on and on with an extensive track record, but once again, this is the MAGA platform, and this is what got people to support it the way that they did. But what really happened? Well, a lot.

ICE (Immigration and Customs Enforcement) under the Trump administration has become more aggressive and expanded beyond its intended scope, prompting critics to question many aspects of its procedures and actions. One of these is multiple deaths tied to ICE, as well as countless cases of unconstitutional, excessive use of their power, which has sparked many questions from the public as to where their boundaries lie, and where the line is drawn when it comes to their stops, as well as the conditions in which detainees had to live when thrown into detention camps. The ACLU conducted a study across 8 states and 1,200 immigration enforcement incidents, in which they found 155 U.S. citizens who were detained, targeted, or otherwise subjected to misconduct by immigration agents. The same review identified 214 children, including 32 U.S. citizens. The question has arisen many times: what is sufficient for an arrest to be made? If you are sitting here thinking, is race sufficient to make a stop or detention? The answer is a very clear no. In United States vs Brignoni-Ponce(1975), the Supreme Court ruled that Border Patrol could not stop a vehicle just on the sole basis that they appeared to be of Mexican ancestry. The court ruled 9-0, holding that race alone was insufficient under the 4th Amendment to establish reasonable suspicion, and that further articulable facts were needed to stop someone. The Supreme Court recognized the dangers of confusing ethnicity and immigration status. The Supreme Court made an emergency ruling in 2025 in the case of Noem v. Vasquez Perdomo, in which they stated race could be added as a factor to the “totality of the circumstances”. Still, it was not sufficient on its own to establish it; this ruling was not a nationwide statement on whether ICE’s practices were constitutional; it was a pause on a lower court's ruling that had restricted certain ICE Immigration stops in Los Angeles. Litigation on this case continued while the lower court's ruling was paused, and Justice Kavanaugh concurred: race alone isn't sufficient to provide reasonable suspicion. But then this raises the question: how many additional factors does the government need to add to ethnicity before it effectively becomes the foundation of the stop? And this is where we see a constitutional grey area that becomes extremely dangerous.

For instance, in Minnesota, in a case brought to the court by the ACLU, alleging that immigration authorities were targeting Latinos and Somalis on suspicionless stops and warrantless arrests. One of the specific examples includes Mubashir Khalif Hussen, a 20-year-old U.S. citizen of Somali ethnicity who claimed he was walking to lunch in Minneapolis' Cedar-Riverside neighborhoods when federal agents approached him, after Mubashir made known multiple times he was a citizen. Still, agents ignored his claims, took him to a federal building, shackled and fingerprinted him, and only released him after it was confirmed he was a citizen. This then begs the question: what was the reason he was stopped, detained, and shackled? This case, along with others compiled, was shown to prove the baseless intent, arguing that the only evidence they had was the race of these people, and, if proven, that is completely against the 4th Amendment, as previously shown. This is one of the many cases that expose the unclear procedures, unconstitutional use of power, and the racial profiling that is so heavily scrutinized and questioned. Now it is only right to look at the detention centers and the conditions in which these people are put into; according to the Human Rights Watch, 52 people have died in ICE custody between January 20, 2025, and June 4, 2026, which is the highest mortality rate in detention centers at any point in nearly 2 decades.

Critics have also questioned ICE’s purpose and methods for who they are trying to get out. Originally, the administration claimed the goal was to get out the worst of the worst. Still, as of April 2026, fewer than 18,000 of more than 60,000 detainees had a previous U.S. criminal conviction. The problem is that when pushing the agenda that all immigrants are violent criminals, nuance disappears, and you fear-monger the public and the uninformed into believing a statement that holds no real proof. Boxing in people that are undocumented, into a category of animals or violent criminals, when violating immigration law is not a criminal offense in itself, and though it can become criminal when you look at the method of entry, labeling these people the way our President continues to do, creates a society in which the administration's supporters will believe and effectively treat people that are immigrants as such, because of the agenda in which are administration and leader of the land pushes. The claim becomes even more baseless when you bring in the fact that studies show in Texas, which has the second-highest immigration population in the country, U.S. citizens are twice as likely to be arrested for violent crimes, 2 and a half times more likely to be arrested for a drug offense, and 4 times more likely to be arrested for property crimes than an undocumented person. You're not just hearing it from me that the agenda and the practices of ICE are problematic; experts estimate judges have ruled against ICE over 10,000 times in a federal court, and data also shows judges have ruled against ICE 90% of the time in cases brought to them about the mandatory detention policies of ICE, with over 400 federal judges across the country having ruled against their practices during the Trump administration. The biggest decision came from the Supreme Court, in which they ruled against Trump and his executive order to limit birthright citizenship; they ruled it was an unconstitutional order, which sparked much more controversy among the people, as well as in his circle, as there were conservative judges, including one of his nominees, who ruled against him in this case. But this once again begs the question: where do we draw the line?

It is not unconstitutional to enforce immigration law, nor is it wrong to want a strong border, secure country, and proper documentation, but at the same time it is right to question racial profiling based on appearance and race, it is right to uphold the Constitution and the right to due process for “all people” as it so clearly states, it is right to want violent criminals and people who are causing harm to be deported from the country and thrown in jail, but recognize that ripping a family apart and throwing them in a detention center isn’t effective, nor is it humane, it is right to want change and transparency when it comes to indefinite detention, inhumane conditions, and imprisonment of people with no criminal records, and it is only right that if you advocate for criminals being deported, you must face the reality our president has been convicted on 34 counts of a felony, who has been held civilly liable for what is now defined as rape in the State of New York, or what was previously defined when the verdict was made as sexual abuse, and it is right to advocate against the use of unnecessary force, and the killing of innocent people. You cannot claim to be pro-life, but when it comes to people that don't look like you, or didn't have the privilege of ending up here like you, you disregard their humanity. But as I have shown, all roads lead back to one thing: blind allegiance and loyalty. No amount of statistics, body cam footage, news headlines, or articles will change the minds of people who are blind and loyal to the movement until they open their eyes and see that this isn’t the America we should want.

Now, it wouldn’t be right if I didn’t talk about one of the most immoral, corrupt things that has essentially, in my opinion, been covered up by this administration, that involves both sides of the political spectrum, and is something that isn’t talked about enough. Jeffrey Epstein, a name that gets thrown around in many conversations about politics, because of the impact one of the most heinous child traffickers and pedophiles had on our political landscape. While a whole separate post could be done on the tricky, unclear relationship of Epstein and Trump, or many of the politicians on both sides, the reason I bring Epstein specifically up, is to bring up his promise which got supporters fired up, which was to release the Epstein files, a collection of documents, emails, pictures, records, reports, etc. related to Epstein, his crimes, and his relations with the elites. Trump repeatedly stated that he would release them, and his former Attorney General, Pam Bondi, said she had the files “on her desk,” ready to review and release them shortly after the Trump administration took office. She said this specifically about Epstein's highly sought-after “client list” and the people he worked with. Now, this sounds great and all right? Exposing pedophiles and the elite class of this country, which has people who are corrupt to the core, is what we need to do, right? The answer is yes, but the reality of it is it wasn’t that simple. Once elected, the first “phase” of the files was released, but it drew much criticism. Most of the content was already released or accessible through a plethora of ways. A little later on, the DOJ released nearly 3.5 million pages of the files in compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which was proposed by Rep. Thomas Massie (R) and Rep. Ro Khanna (D) and voted on and enacted by Congress. Many problems arose: the first was that nearly 200,000 pages were completely redacted, or information was heavily withheld; individual files requested were also completely redacted, including emails and indictments that contained the names of co-conspirators. On top of all this, there were files in which the victims' names were unredacted, and the conspirators were redacted, which sparked outrage among people, as this is completely contradictory to why the files needed to be released in the first place. Not a single person has been prosecuted or arrested in coordination with the release of the files.

The other major problem is the Transparency Act, which Trump signed, gave the DOJ 30 days to release all the files. He signed this in November of 2025; I am writing this in August of 2026, and investigators estimate that 2.5 to 3 million of the files we still don’t have are being withheld from the public. An investigation by NPR also found that the DOJ withheld files that contained allegations involving Trump. After the release of the investigation, Rep. Robert Garcia, D-Calif released a statement saying, “Yesterday, I reviewed unredacted evidence logs at the Department of Justice. Oversight Democrats can confirm that the DOJ appears to have illegally withheld FBI interviews with this survivor who accused President Trump of heinous crimes.” The DOJ later released some of the withheld files, but some still have not been released. It is also important to note that Trump's name has been mentioned over 38,000 times in the Epstein files, according to the Times. However, this does not mean he is guilty; it is merely allegations. No common person should pass over the fact that our President is implicated 38,000 times in coordination with one of the worst child predators in history. Not only that, but remember how I mentioned Trump's openness to releasing the files during his campaign? Well, in 2025, and into the present date, Trump has called the files a democrat hoax, and has questioned reporters on why we are still talking about the files as a whole, and attacking his supporters who want transparency, saying they are undermining what Trump has done for this country. I think to someone who is somewhat in the right mindset, calling a investigation and evidence that has to do with the abuse, assault, trafficking, and rape of little girls, a “hoax” should immediately be a massive red flag, when months prior they pledged to release and give transparency on these topics, and stated they had the names of the conspirators on the desk waiting to be reviewed. Still, months later, they claim there was never a list. Which one is it?

Trump's behavior towards politicians on his side, whom he pushed to be transparent, was also deeply disturbing. Thomas Massie's downfall with the President became most noticeable when he presented the Transparency Act, which I previously mentioned. This added fuel to the fire on what was already an ongoing plethora of disagreements between the President and Congressman Massie, resulting in Trump calling for a new candidate that he backed to take Massie's seat, which eventually happened in what would end up being the most expensive U.S. House primary in history, amounting to more than 18 million of outside PAC funding for Gallrein’s side alone. Trump called him a “lowlife” after he pushed for the files to be released, as well as many other rude names and labels. Don’t you think it’s kind of interesting that Trump heavily backed Massie for a great deal of time, but when it came to exposing pedophiles and funds sent to Israel to commit genocide, as well as some economic policies, Trump did everything in his power to remove him as fast as possible? Massie has since continued the fight for greater transparency and more releases, exposing corruption within the administration.

Marjorie Taylor Greene arguably had a more direct role in her resignation from office, according to the files, as she was only one of four Republicans to sign the discharge petition to force a vote on Epstein. After doing so, Trump called her a “weak congresswoman," “a ranting lunatic,” and a “traitor,” as well as during a Tucker Carlson interview, Massie himself stated that Marjorie told him that Trump allegedly told her if she released these files, it would hurt his friends. Marjorie also stated in a hearing alongside Massie and some of the Epstein survivors that she believes the President's attacks came directly from her petitioning for a vote on the release of the files. Trump broke ties with her, and once again, said he would back a new candidate in the primary. All of this led Marjorie to effectively end her political career for the time being, as she resigned from Congress on January 5th, 2026. Seeing a pattern here? Asking for transparency, signing for transparency, President turns on you, cuts ties and endorsements with you, kinda seems like someone who doesn’t want transparency, right?

Well if you're not convinced yet, take once again another Congresswoman Nancy Mace, who before her fall out with the President, self proclaimed herself as “Trump in high heels”, and by this quote alone, you can tell that this wasn’t someone who was on the conservative side, and wasn’t just a big fan of Trump, this was someone who genuinely wanted to embody Trump and his policies. Mace, like the two others I’ve mentioned, was one of the 4 Republicans to vote for the discharge to vote on the Epstein Files. There is evidence of direct pressure from Trump and his administration to get Boebert and Mace to remove their names from the discharge, but Mace, alongside Boebert, ultimately refused. In this instance, while the results are clear, it was a little trickier because Trump didn’t specifically attack Mace after verbally endorsing this decision. Still, it is one of those cases in which the results and actions can speak for themselves. Mace then ran for Governor of South Carolina and sought Trump’s gubernatorial endorsement in the primary, in which Trump endorsed her opponent, Lt. Gov. Pamela Evette. Mace finished 5th in this election, with about 12% of the vote. She later stated after her loss, “I voted to release the Epstein files and lost some support for that." Whether her vote on the files was the reason she ultimately lost her political career is debatable, but I know that a President who tries to convince people in his own party not to vote for the release of the Epstein files is someone who needs to be questioned.

A coincidence starts becoming less likely when coincidences turn into patterns, and those patterns all share multiple facts in common. The facts are these: there were 3 people previously endorsed by Trump, and all 3 signed the discharge to allow a vote on the release of the files. Subsequently, all three lost their endorsements from Trump, lost their primaries, or resigned. All three believe that their signing on the discharge had something to do with this fallout, while two of them were publicly and directly scrutinized by Trump. So readers, when do we stop putting up with the BS? When do people start to realize there's more to the story, and when do they realize this isn't something we should let slip under the covers?

But all roads lead to home, right? Blind allegiance. When you can get an audience to defend the fact that evidence strongly shows you're covering up, and even implicated (though this does not mean he’s automatically guilty) in some of the most heinous acts and trafficking rings in recent times, you have blind allegiance. You have loyalty like no other. And you have a movement where the numbness to personal accountability becomes so strong, it practically doesn’t exist anymore. If no amount of pictures, flight logs, letters, emails, witness statements, prior comments, prior charges of sexual abuse, or the blatant lies and contradictory statements can convince someone they need to reevaluate the candidate they voted for, then the mindset implied there is one that is extremely dangerous for our society as a whole on both sides of the political spectrum. Loyalty isn't a bad thing inherently; everyone is loyal politically to the ideas in which they believe are just, but it becomes a slippery slope when the loyalty numbs you to logic and reason, and even more dangerous when it closes your eyes to corruption. That type of loyalty looks like raiding the Capitol building and causing terror, and then getting pardoned by the president for how you were wrongfully “treated”.

So I would like to leave you with this. The moment allegiance to a leader supersedes the law and reason, democracy will cease to be a government run by principles, but a government of devotion to the idea of what someone said they would be. A mere idea.
